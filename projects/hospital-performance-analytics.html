The business problem

What factors help explain differences in hospital performance and quality outcomes?

Why it mattered

Hospitals report dozens of overlapping metrics. Decision makers need to know which few underlying factors actually separate strong performers from weak ones.

My role

I worked on exploratory analysis, standardization and interpretation of principal components to explain relationships among hospital measures.

Data and tools

  • More than 100,000 healthcare records containing financial, operational, and patient-experience measures; original dataset citation to be confirmed
  • Exploratory data analysis and Principal Component Analysis

How I approached it

  • Explored the data with statistical methods to check distributions and relationships.
  • Applied Principal Component Analysis to combine correlated variables into a small set of underlying performance factors.
  • Interpreted the components and linked them to quality outcomes.
  • Presented insights as part of the Business Intelligence and Insights course at Hult.

Findings

One interpreted component was associated with hospital scale and financial variables; another reflected patient experience and communication measures. These dimensions should not be treated as equivalent.

Business implications

Use financial/scale indicators and patient-experience measures as complementary performance dimensions rather than assuming that strength in one guarantees strength in the other.

Limitations

  • The analysis shows association, not cause.
  • Principal components are blends of variables, so they need careful naming before a non technical audience can use them.
  • Quality and patient experience measures can reflect expectations as well as care.

What this shows

Handling a large real world dataset, simplifying a messy set of metrics with PCA, and being careful about what correlation can support.
