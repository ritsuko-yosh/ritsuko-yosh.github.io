The business problem

Take a product idea, Posture Mate, and decide whether it could become a viable business. Posture Mate is a concept I created for the fellowship. No product was built or sold.

Why it mattered

Most product ideas fail on economics, not on the idea. The point was to test whether this one could make money before anyone spent money on it.

My role

I created the concept and built the business case as part of the Open Avenues Foundation Build Fellowship, led by Nani Detti.

Data and tools

  • Market and competitor research and planning assumptions developed for an academic entrepreneurship exercise; individual source references are not included here

How I approached it

  • Market research and competitor analysis to define the customer and the alternatives.
  • Pricing, manufacturing, logistics, and distribution assumptions to see whether each unit could be profitable at scale.
  • A three year financial outlook to estimate funding needs.
  • Recommendations on positioning, go to market, and growth.

Findings

The business case evaluated how pricing, manufacturing, logistics, and distribution assumptions affect potential commercial feasibility; precise estimates are not published without the supporting model.

Business implications

The concept required further validation of customer demand and supplier costs before making a launch or funding decision.

Limitations

  • All figures are projections for a concept that was never launched.
  • Cost assumptions were not tested against real supplier quotes.

What this shows

Commercial judgment: connecting customer, competition, price, and cost into one view of whether a business works.
